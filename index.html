💡 Storage Takeaway: The 85% vs. 3% Footprint Disparity

Framed (245) and XL (37) works account for >83% of the total storage footprint (~1,075 cu. ft.) and drive almost all monthly rent. In contrast, all 246 Unframed Prints occupy less than 16 sq. ft. (two architectural flat file cabinets).

Implication: Placing or liquidating 40–50 large framed pieces immediately cuts storage overhead in half, while preserving all 246 prints costs almost nothing.

Posthumous Transmission: In Jim's Voice

Dictated from the Afterlife (or wherever they put people who made too many Nike commercials)
Archival Persona

Okay. I’m dead now. You saw the Instagram post. It was in white Helvetica on a black square, because if you’re going to announce you’ve checked out after sixty-six years, you might as well use a typeface that doesn’t beg for sympathy. Sympathy is for people who didn’t get to spend two decades playing with plastic figurines of Adolf Hitler.

For twenty-four years, cancer tried to kill me. Chronic myelogenous leukemia, prostate cancer, chemotherapy, radiation, pills that made me look like an over-boiled turnip. All it really accomplished was giving me an ironclad excuse to quit advertising. I went from a career of selling people things they don't need to making things that people don't want. It was the best trade of my life.

People used to ask me why I put dictators in bunny ears or had Hermann Göring eat sausages on a doily. Because dictators are pompous, pathetic bullies. And you don’t destroy a bully with a stern academic thesis. You point at his ridiculous boots, put a jester hat on his head, and laugh until he shrinks down to the size of a Happy Meal toy.

Now I leave behind 578 pieces of art in various flat files, vaults, and drawer units between Portland and San Francisco. Let’s make one thing abundantly clear to Jake, Haley, Peter, Guy, and Benji: Do not put this work in an ivory tower. Do not treat it like precious, untouchable holy relics.

Send the invoices to the foreign embassies. Charge the dictators for twenty-four years of childcare and plastic upkeep. Prank the agencies. Sneak toys into the Cannes Lion trophy cases. And if some billionaire with more money than sense wants to pay fifty thousand dollars for a framed picture of Joseph Stalin in pink boxing shorts, take his check, give half of it to the cancer doctors at OHSU so someone else can survive twenty-four years, and spend the other half on a decent lunch.

Overview & Encyclopedic Biography

Wikipedia, The One Club Hall of Fame, and estate archival records

James Paul Riswold (December 7, 1957 – August 9, 2024) was an acclaimed American creative director for Wieden+Kennedy and later a prolific satirical fine artist. In his 14 years at W+K, he conceived legendary campaigns including Michael Jordan and Spike Lee (Mars Blackmon), Bugs Bunny & Hare Jordan, Bo Jackson’s "Bo Knows", and Tiger Woods’ "I Am Tiger Woods". In 2013, he was inducted into The One Club’s Creative Hall of Fame.

Following a diagnosis of leukemia in 2000, Riswold retired from advertising to dedicate his life to fine art, remarking that he moved from "a career of selling people things they don't need to making things that people don't want."

His photographic and sculptural work poked fierce fun at history’s most taboo dictators, tyrants, and monumental figures—including Adolf Hitler, Hermann Göring, Joseph Stalin, Mao Zedong, and Vladimir Putin. By staging small toy figurines in solemn, museum-scale environments, Riswold deflated megalomania: "Instead of providing grand expositions mythologizing the dictator, toys, by definition, make their subjects seem small, childish, and trifling."

Following his passing in 2024, his estate is actively managed by his children, Jake Riswold and Haley Riswold, in collaboration with long-time gallery partners Peter Yue and Guy Overfelt.
