💡 Storage Takeaway: The 85% vs. 3% Footprint Disparity

Framed (245) and XL (37) works account for >83% of the total storage footprint (~1,075 cu. ft.) and drive almost all monthly rent. In contrast, all 246 Unframed Prints occupy less than 16 sq. ft. (two architectural flat file cabinets).

Implication: Placing or liquidating 40–50 large framed pieces immediately cuts storage overhead in half, while preserving all 246 prints costs almost nothing.

Overview & Encyclopedic Biography

Wikipedia, The One Club Hall of Fame, and estate archival records

James Paul Riswold (December 7, 1957 – August 9, 2024) was an acclaimed American creative director for Wieden+Kennedy and later a prolific satirical fine artist. In his 14 years at W+K, he conceived legendary campaigns including Michael Jordan and Spike Lee (Mars Blackmon), Bugs Bunny & Hare Jordan, Bo Jackson’s "Bo Knows", and Tiger Woods’ "I Am Tiger Woods". In 2013, he was inducted into The One Club’s Creative Hall of Fame.

Following a diagnosis of leukemia in 2000, Riswold retired from advertising to dedicate his life to fine art, remarking that he moved from "a career of selling people things they don't need to making things that people don't want."

His photographic and sculptural work poked fierce fun at history’s most taboo dictators, tyrants, and monumental figures—including Adolf Hitler, Hermann Göring, Joseph Stalin, Mao Zedong, and Vladimir Putin. By staging small toy figurines in solemn, museum-scale environments, Riswold deflated megalomania: "Instead of providing grand expositions mythologizing the dictator, toys, by definition, make their subjects seem small, childish, and trifling."

Following his passing in 2024, his estate is actively managed by his children, Jake Riswold and Haley Riswold, in collaboration with long-time gallery partners Peter Yue and Guy Overfelt.
